### Video transcript: Inclusive spaces

Narrator Meg:

Inclusive spaces are about more than physical accessibility. They're about creating environments where everyone feels welcome, safe, and able to participate fully. This includes considering the layout of workspaces, lighting, noise levels, quiet areas, and the design of both physical and digital environments.

When spaces are designed with only one type of user in mind, barriers can arise. Whether it's a colleague who uses a wheelchair, someone who's neurodivergent and easily overwhelmed by noise, or a person with a visual impairment, who relies on tactile cues, inclusive spaces recognise and accommodate those diverse needs, ensuring that colleagues can move, interact, and contribute comfortably.

In this section we'll explore practical strategies for creating accessible, flexible, and welcoming workplaces. From physical design to digital accessibility to fostering inclusive behaviours and culture, we'll give you the guidance to help ensure all colleagues feel respected and supported and enabled to do their best work.

### Inclusive workspaces: the built environment

Creating an accessible physical space is all about multi-functionality and adaptability to personal preference. It's about considering the things that many of us don't notice or think about on our routes to and from and around our workplaces, but that have a huge impact on people's experience of the world. Navigate through the tabs below to find out some key areas to look out for when building inclusive environments:

## Interactive: tabs

### Access routes

Have you ever thought about how diverse individuals might get to and from, and into the spaces you're using? As a good starting point, consider:

- Entranceways: are they wide enough for mobility aids like wheelchairs? Are they power assisted so that they're not too heavy? Are they clear and uncluttered?

- Routes to and from the building: is there an accessible way to get in and out of the building? Are there ramps? Are there&nbsp;clear pedestrian pathways? Is the terrain safe and even?

- Accessible parking: Are there designated parking spaces that are wide enough for wheelchairs? Are there safe routes from the car park to the building?

- Companions: Are there easily accessible places where companions like guide dogs or hearing assistance dogs can go to the toilet?

### Navigation and way finding

Buildings can quickly turn into confusing and overwhelming labyrinths when they're not clearly laid out and signposted. As a start, consider:

- Signage: Are the signs that help you navigate around the building clear, large, and in multiple formats like braille?

- Internal layout: Is the building cohesively laid out, intuitive to use, and inclusive of accessible spaces like quiet spaces, accessible break and kitchen facilities with easy to find supplies?

- Multi-functional spaces: can you spaces be rearranged to suit individual access needs and preference?

- Induction: Have you provided a virtual, video, or in-person&nbsp;tour for new starters before their first day, so that they can accustom to navigating the space quickly?

### Accessible bathrooms

Accessible bathrooms are a basic human right, and should be able to be used with dignity and ease. As a starting point, consider:

- Physical layout: is the bathroom wide enough for a wheelchair, having appropriate transfer space next to the toilet, with handrails and an emergency cord?

- Clutter: is the accessible bathroom clean, clear, and clutter free - we often see these spaces being used as storage which is inappropriate

- &nbsp;Are you bathrooms inclusive of intersectional needs: For example, do you have gender inclusive options, options with grab locks and handles rather than small dexterity options etc

### Meetings and events

When planning your meetings and events, have you considered multiple access needs, and asked your diverse colleagues upfront what support they may require to attend? As a starting point, consider:

- Have you looked into the accessibility standards &nbsp;&nbsp;the event space you are considering? Does it meet your essential criteria?

- Does the event space have step-free access, as well as accessible public transport options to get to and from the venue?

- Have you considered break-out spaces so that people can desensitise from overwhelming events?

- Have you considered intersectional needs like prayer spaces, child-nursing spaces and spaces where dietary inclusive food options are available?

A is correct because inclusive spaces anticipate diverse needs from the start. Accessibility shouldn’t be reactive or optional—it’s part of building an environment where everyone can participate equally.

- **Correct answer:** A. Ensure the venue has step-free access, quiet areas, clear signage, and accessible toilets, and share this information with attendees in advance.

- Answer option: B. Skip accessibility planning since the event is temporary.

- Answer option: C. Only provide adjustments if someone complains on the day.
