### Video transcript: workplace adjustments

Narrator Meghan: &nbsp;

Adjustments are changes made to remove barriers so that everyone can contribute and thrive at work. Adjustments support people, they aren't extra, they're essential. You might also hear them referred to as reasonable adjustments or workplace accommodations, or perhaps support measures or inclusive practices.

But really, adjustments are just about making the workplace accessible and inclusive. To do this, we must take an equitable approach. Equity acknowledges that people experience different barriers and therefore need tailored, personalised accommodations to access the workplace equally. We don't all need access to a specific adjustment like an alternative mouse, but for those who do, providing the right device or adjustment enables them to participate on an equal basis with their colleagues.

However, if we were to provide that adapted mouse for everyone as standard as the only option, just because it was fair or equal, it wouldn't work and it would likely even have a negative impact. That's the importance of equity and workplace adjustments. Many adjustments can be made with little or no cost to the company, with positive and inclusive changes made by creating a safe space where people can share their access needs, try new things or figure out what works best for them, and ultimately for the whole team.

Types of adjustments

Changes to policies, procedures or practices
e.g. adaptations to the recruitment process (e.g. interview questions in advance), flexible working policies, modified ways of working, or creating more inclusive policies.

Provision of equipment, services or support
e.g. ergonomic equipment to prevent injury or strain, specialist assistive technology such as speech to text software, and human support, such as coaching or training.

Changes to the physical environment
e.g. installing lifts and accessible bathrooms, providing adjustable lighting and quiet areas to work in, and providing accessible parking close to the store or office.

## Multimedia: audio

Optional voiceover

Audio: `63b73976-585e-4e17-802a-48.mp3` (42 seconds; present)

**Embedded narration script:**

Common misconceptions:

Myth: Adjustments are expensive and difficult to implement
Fact: Most adjustments cost little to nothing, e.g. simple changes like flexible working hours

Myth: Once adjustments are made, they will fix all of the challenges and don't need to be reviewed
Fact: Access needs can change over time, and adjustments may need to be altered. It should be an ongoing communication

Myth: Adjustments are only relevant if someone has a formal diagnosis
Fact: Many people will still experience barriers, even if they don't have an official diagnosis. Plus, inclusive practice benefits everyone!
