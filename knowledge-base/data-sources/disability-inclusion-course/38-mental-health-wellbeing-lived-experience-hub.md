### Video transcript: Solène's experience

Solène:

Hey everyone, my name is Solène Anglaret and my pronouns are she/her. I'm a white woman in her late thirties, and today I'm wearing a white T-shirt and a blue and white scarf. Next to me, there's a cute green plant, and behind me there's a light gray curtain. I'm a consultant, coach and creative, and the founder of Be Beyond Borders.

Before that, I spent over 12 years working in large corporations and government organisations across seven different countries. I also have lived experience of neurodivergence, ADHD, and mental health, BPD, or borderline personality disorder. So today I'm gonna be talking to you about some of the barriers that I faced in the workplace, and adjustments that can be put in place so that I and people who are similar to me can thrive.

So let's start with some of the barriers. The first set is around authenticity and belonging. I often felt like I couldn't be myself in the workplace and I needed to hide. Why? Because I was told that I need to be less sensitive or more assertive. The other aspect is I felt pigeonholed a lot of the time.

I was told, here's your job description and you need to stick to that, when actually I like to do so many different things and contribute in different ways. The next set of barriers is around workload and structure. Anything to do with rigidity is very challenging, so rigid office hours or having to be at the same place physically present in an office every day.

Also handling tasks that feel repetitive or meaningless or where I don't see any opportunity to learn and grow. Last minute changes, requests like that can create a lot of panic for me as well. And often I've worked in environments where overworking was the norm and I felt like I had to overwork to prove my worth, which led to burnout, stress, and had a really negative impact on my mental and physical health.

Third set of barriers is around management and communication. I personally don't respond well to micromanagement, and I prefer a lot of independence and autonomy. I can also struggle with conflicts, and so I need my time away to regulate in that kind of situation and feedback can be difficult for me both giving and receiving.

So knowing all of this, what kind of adjustments can help me thrive? First off, flexibility and time. I started working four days a week. I then worked also compressed hours, which is five days into four, and that made a huge difference 'cause I had time for my own passion projects outside of work and I could be super productive in the days that I was working.

Hybrid working made a really positive difference as well, like having the ability to mix, working from home, working from the office. That's been great. Clear deadlines and agendas sent in advance. Super helpful. Also, having buffer time between meetings, so making meetings 45 minutes or 50 minutes instead of a full hour, and really honoring that so that there is a bit of time for task switching in between.

The second set of adjustments is around communication and structure. It is really much better for me to receive emails or messages instead of sudden phone calls. Again, people in your team might be different. How insightful would it be to have that conversation openly. And also around performance. Making sure to measure performance based on delivery and not purely on presence or who shouts loudest.

Final set of adjustments is around teamwork and collaboration. Having the ability to swap tasks between members of the team so that we can all play to our strengths. Or another example of that is working with a manager that balances well independence and support. So as you've heard, a lot of these things don't cost any money, and they're really small and [00:05:00] simple changes and shifts that everyone can make. So let's go a little bit further into that. What are some of the small things that can make a very big difference? First around management style, integrity, and honesty.

Do what you say, say what you do. Asking if I need help, especially if I look flustered or tired, I might not dare to ask. So if you ask me, that's really, really helpful. Providing encouragement to take breaks and breathe, and also if you can, removing any unnecessary office politics. I'm really not good at that.

Second set is around feedback and recognition. If I'm doing a good job, tell me positive reinforcements based on real actual contributions goes such a long way. If you need to or would like to give me some constructive feedback, the best way is to start first by asking, is this the right time? Is this a good place to do it? And finally, having regular check-ins and open conversations about mental health. Let's talk about it.

Final set is everyday interactions. That's valid for everyone, colleagues, managers, everybody around in the team. See and lean into my strengths. Make space for emotions. Yours, mine, without any judgment. Offer gentle reminders if I've forgotten something sometimes and invite me to help others. I love being useful.

I love contributing to the team. I love adding value. Lean into that and I won't disappoint.

To conclude everyone's needs will be different, but these examples are small, thoughtful actions and adjustments that we can all make and that have a huge impact. With the right support and culture, people like me, myself included, with lived experience of neurodivergence and mental health, can thrive and bring amazing strengths into the workplace.

Thank you so much for listening.
