### Video transcript: Introduction to diverse speech

Narrator Meghan:

We don't all sound the same when we speak, and that's part of human diversity. Whether it's an accent, a stammer, a varied vocal pattern linked to disability, or neurodivergence, or speech generated through a talker or AAC device. Diverse speech can enrich communication. Yet, it is often misunderstood and stigmatised due to bias and traditional views of what communication should or shouldn't include, especially in a professional or workplace environment.

Diverse speech may affect how quickly or easily someone expresses themselves, but it doesn't diminish the clarity, depth, or value of their ideas. Challenges often come not from the speech difference themselves, but from the environment where people are interrupted, dismissed, or pressured to conform to a standard way of communicating. In this module we'll challenge assumptions, share how diverse speech can be an asset, and help you to listen with openness and curiosity.

We'll learn about the common misconceptions, the common barriers, and we'll hear lived experience accounts of why inclusion in the workplace in relation to speech differences is so fundamental.

## Text: subheading paragraph

### Overcoming the stigma

There is still extreme bias and discriminatory attitudes in all areas of our society about speech differences being an indication of a lack of intelligence or ability, which simply isn't the case. In fact, in a study of 232 adults, over 70% felt they had been turned down for hiring at interview, or promotion, due to their speech differences, and 56% reported having experienced at least one “major discrimination event” during their life because of their stuttering (NIH).

## Text: paragraph

Stamma is a UK based charity whose work focuses on raising awareness, providing practical tools, and driving systemic change to make society and workplaces more inclusive for people who stammer. Their posters highlight one of the most important barriers that people with diverse speech face: interruption. People with diverse speech have clear, intelligent and detailed thoughts that they want to express, and often just need a little more time to formulate the words, and it can be an incredibly frustrating experience for people to try to guess at finishing sentences for them. Even if it's well intentioned, it can be distracting, make people lose their train of thought, or even just make people feel like their contributions have been validated. Next time: just have a little patience!

## Image: text aside

Illustration of two people talking, with various speech bubbles around them

Top tips for supporting colleagues with diverse speech

- Listen patiently, don’t interrupt – Give colleagues time to finish their thoughts without jumping in or completing sentences for them.

- Normalise different speech styles – Focus on the content of what’s being said, not how it’s said.

- Provide flexible communication options – Offer alternatives like written responses, chat functions, or follow-up emails where helpful.

- Create inclusive meeting practices – Allow extra time for contributions, avoid putting people on the spot, and use round-robin or hand-raising to manage turn-taking.

- Challenge stigma and assumptions – Model respect, avoid making jokes or comments about speech, and encourage a culture where diverse communication is valued.

- Answer option: C. Offering written alternatives for communication.
