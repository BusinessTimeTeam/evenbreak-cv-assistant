### Video transcript: supporting mental health in the workplace

Narrator Meghan:

Mental health is something that we all have. Just like physical health. At times we may feel well and resilient while at other times stress, anxiety, or depression or other experiences might affect how we think, feel, and work. Facing challenges with mental health is part of being human, and yet stigma and misunderstanding still create barriers for many people in the workplace.

Mental health impacts vary widely, and people's experiences are deeply individual. Some may live with long-term conditions, others might experience shorter periods of challenge, and some will move and fluctuate between times of wellness and ill health. Importantly, good workplace culture, open communication and accessible support can make a real difference to how people can manage and thrive at work.

In this section we'll explore common myths, misconceptions about mental health, and we'll highlight the importance of early support and open dialogue. We'll also share best practice strategies that help create a workplace where mental wellbeing is valued, protected, and supported.

## Text: subheading

### Factors influencing mental health

## List: bulleted

Individual factors can include things like past trauma, addiction, access to support, disability or neurodivergence, and personal values/beliefs.

Social factors can include unemployment, increased cost of living, access to food and housing, access to education and meaningful activity, exposure to stigma and discrimination.

Work factors can include high pressured, stressful environments, lack of progression and job satisfaction, fear of exclusion and punishment.

Talking about wellbeing

Talking about mental health, and offering support for those who need it, can feel daunting. But it's important to make sure people are heard and safe to open up when experiencing challenges. Here's some top tips for language choices when discussing sensitive and personal information:

Do's:

Use open questions, and give the person plenty of time and space to answer.

Have regular check-in's with the individual to create continual support and ensure they're well enough to be in work.

Reassure the individual - it's okay to ask for help.

Dedicate actual time to listen actively and compassionately. These conversations should never feel rushed.

Don'ts:

Invalidate or judge the other person's feelings as this will make them close down.

Use cliche phrases, like 'we all have bad days', 'time heals all wounds', or 'just smile, it'll make you feel better'.
Push the individual to talk before they are ready.

Diagnose the individual or act like a therapist unless you're a trained mental health professional.

Make assumptions about their trauma.

Strategies for supporting wellbeing

- Understand capacity: recognise when you and others are reaching a limit, feeling overwhelmed, or approaching burnout.

- Shift around projects and deadlines to make sure no one person is carrying too much.

- Implement workplace adjustments like flexible hours, increased breaks, and time off if needed.

- Make sure employees are supported e.g. plan and prioritise tasks with them, provide access to coaching and mentoring.

- Know where to sign-post to: Have resources, crisis lines, and occupational health numbers on hand if you think an employee is at risk.

## Knowledgecheck: multiple choice

### Which of the following best describes the difference between mental health and wellbeing?

Correct answer: C – Mental health relates to our thoughts, feelings, and behaviours, while wellbeing focuses on resilience, purpose, and overall life satisfaction.

- Answer option: A. Mental health is about resilience, while wellbeing is about diagnosing illness.

- Answer option: B. Mental health and wellbeing are the same, so either term can be used interchangeably.

- **Correct answer:** C. Mental health is about our emotional, psychological, and social state; wellbeing is about building resilience and living a life of purpose.

- Answer option: D. Wellbeing focuses only on work performance, while mental health focuses on personal life.

## Knowledgecheck: multiple choice

### True or false? Talking openly about mental health in the workplace is always helpful, even if the person isn’t ready to share.

Correct answer: False – Pressuring someone to talk before they’re ready can make them withdraw or feel judged, which is counterproductive.

- Answer option: True

- **Correct answer:** False

## Knowledgecheck: multiple choice

### You notice a team member is showing signs of stress and challenges coping. Which of the following actions is most appropriate?

Correct answer: D – Providing compassionate support, listening actively, and signposting resources helps the person feel safe and understood without overstepping boundaries.

- Answer option: A. Push them to talk immediately about what they are experiencing.

- Answer option: B. Diagnose their experience and give advice.

- Answer option: C. Tell them “we all have bad days” to make them feel better.

- **Correct answer:** D. Offer support by checking in, listening actively, and suggesting available resources if they need help.
