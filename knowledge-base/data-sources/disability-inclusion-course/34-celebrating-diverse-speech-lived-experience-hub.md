Hi I'm Josh and I work at Evenbreak and I have a speech impediment also known as

a stutter. This can be described as a disruption in the flow of speaking and it can be categorised as blocks which is when there's a moment of a silence and there's no language coming out and there's repetitions which are like counts of elongations which can be sound for example. In terms of barriers when applying for work, I think for myself personally, reading a job advert that says something along the words of completely fluent sometimes has previously made me hesitant to apply as that's not something I can guarantee any person let alone any company.

Also a lot of initial communication can happen over a telephone call and I think personally having those non verbal cues not only for myself but also for the listener is key because there are certain parts of my condition that mean that how the person walks into a conversation

&nbsp;with the expectation of how it will go will completely change once there's a moment of disfluency. So I previously requested having a video call rather than a telephone call and I've also had a chance to request more time in an interview because it's not the case that I don't know what I'm trying to say to other people it's just a case of it might take me longer to get my words out in that moment.

In terms of adjustments I've never really asked for any in terms of in the workplace, once I've got a job it kind of comes down to how my team and the people around me can support. Just having patience is key which I know is probably quite an easy one to recognise but not everyone has it. I think it's a case even though it might come from very good intentions try not to complete what people are going to say, because if you end up saying the wrong word it means that that person has to go through the whole process of having trouble getting that word out.

I also think it's important to remember that this is a condition where it can vary at any point in time and in that regard I mean that I can be really fluent one moment and at another time it's really hard to get my words out and I think that ties in to that just having patience and letting that person talk, so I think in terms of main takeaway points, be patient with that person. Remember that their speech impediment, that isn’t just the whole part of that person and it may be at the forefront in interviews, because obviously it’s a time pressured situation, but the person knows what they're trying to say too.

That’s kind of all of the points, but I’d just like to caveat, to say that this is my experience, because obviously the only way to help the person that you’re trying to support is to open up a friendly conversation with that person and have that conversation so you can put whatever comes out of it in place.
