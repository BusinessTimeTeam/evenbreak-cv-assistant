# Creating an accessibl

Creating an Accessible Built Environment
A best practice guide from Evenbreak
Introduction
Practical tips for making workplaces physically accessible for all employees and visitors.
A truly inclusive workplace starts with an environment that everyone can enter, navigate, and use without barriers. Accessibility is not just a legal requirement, it’s essential for enabling people to work, collaborate, and thrive.
These tips are based on best practice, Part M of the Building Regulations (Access to and use of buildings) in the UK, and inclusive design principles.
Entrances and access routes
Approach and pathways
Provide a step-free route from the site boundary, car park, or drop-off point to the entrance.
	•	Minimum 1500mm clear width for main approach paths to allow two wheelchair users to pass.
	•	Firm, slip-resistant, and even surface on approach routes.
	•	Avoid steep gradients; maximum gradient 1:20 for ramped approaches where possible.
	•	Keep routes free of obstacles (e.g. bins, planters, A-boards).
Ramps and steps
Where ramps are required, ensure:
	•	Maximum gradient 1:12 (short ramps) or 1:20 (long ramps) in line with Part M.
	•	Minimum 1200mm clear width.
	•	Landings at top and bottom at least 1500mm long.
	•	Handrails on both sides at heights of 900mm and 600mm.
If steps are present, provide contrasting nosings and tactile hazard warning surfaces at the top and bottom.
Entrance features
	•	Covered entrance canopy to protect from weather where possible.
	•	Adequate lighting at the entrance (minimum 100 lux).
	•	Non-reflective, high-contrast door surfaces to aid visibility.
	•	Glazed doors fitted with manifestation (two contrasting bands at 850mm–1000mm and 1400mm–1600mm above floor level).
Extra things to consider:
If an intercom or access control system is used:
	•	Position between 900mm–1200mm from the ground.
	•	Provide visual, audible, and tactile feedback.
Ensure revolving doors are never the sole entrance, always provide an adjacent accessible entrance.
Signage
General placement and positioning
	•	Position signs consistently throughout the building at decision points, entrances, and key facilities.
	•	Mount signs at a consistent height (typically between 1400mm–1700mm from floor level) for readability by wheelchair users and standing users.
	•	Place signs at the beginning and end of corridors, near lifts, staircases, and exits.
Tactile and Braille
Include tactile raised letters for key room names, numbers, and directions. Provide Grade 1 or Grade 2 Braille below printed text. Ensure tactile and Braille content follows accessibility guidelines.
Design and visibility
	•	Use high contrast between text and background – minimum 70% contrast ratio.
	•	Choose sans serif fonts (e.g. Arial, Helvetica, Verdana) for better legibility.
	•	Avoid all capitals for body text; use sentence case or Title Case.
	•	Ensure text size is large enough to be read from intended viewing distance (e.g. minimum 16pt for close-up, 50mm high letters for distance reading).
	•	Avoid reflective or glossy finishes that can cause glare.
Symbols and pictograms
Use clear, internationally recognised symbols (e.g. wheelchair symbol, hearing loop). Position symbols consistently to the left of text for easier scanning.
Internal layout and navigation
	•	Provide continuous, step-free circulation between all key areas (meeting rooms, toilets, kitchens). 
	•	Maintain a minimum corridor width of 1.2m (1.5m preferred) for comfortable passing. 
	•	Install contrasting colour strips on door frames and edges for partially sighted people. 
	•	Use clear, large-print signage (minimum 16–18pt font) in high contrast, with pictograms where possible. 
	•	Provide tactile/Braille signage on room names and lift buttons.
Furniture and workspace layout
	•	Arrange desks and chairs to maintain 1.2m-wide circulation spaces.
	•	Ensure adjustable-height desks are available.
	•	Provide accessible meeting tables with 700mm minimum knee clearance.
	•	Avoid fixed seating arrangements.
Lighting and acoustics
	•	Provide even, glare-free lighting throughout.
	•	Use acoustic panels or carpeting to reduce echo in open spaces.
	•	Ensure emergency lighting is accessible and clear in both visual and audible formats.
Lifts and vertical movement
If the building has more than one floor, provide a passenger lift that meets Part M:
	•	Minimum internal dimensions: 1100mm x 1400mm. 
	•	Doors at least 800mm wide.
	•	Position lift controls 900–1200mm from the floor and in contrasting colours. Provide audible floor announcements and visual indicators.
	•	Doors should remain open for a minimum of 5 seconds to allow safe entry/exit.
Extra things to consider:
	•	Always place controls on the same side as the door opening to avoid confusion.
	•	Include tactile buttons/numbers and Braille.
	•	Ensure the emergency intercom has a visual indicator confirming it’s working.
	•	Install handrails on at least one side of the lift at 900mm height.
	•	Use bright, non-glare lighting inside the lift.
	•	Choose slip-resistant, low-glare flooring for safety.
	•	Ensure the flooring has sufficient visual contrast to help distinguish the lift floor from surrounding areas.
	•	Keep lift foyers clear of obstacles and clutter.
	•	Ensure evacuation plans include procedures for employees and visitors who cannot use stairs.
Bathrooms
Part M of the UK Building Regulations ensures bathrooms are accessible for all users, including:
	•	Wheelchair users.
	•	Ambulant disabled people.
	•	People with mobility, sensory, and cognitive differences.
	•	This includes toilet layout, fittings, access routes, signage, and usability.
Wheelchair-accessible toilet (unisex)
	•	Minimum 2200mm (length) x 1500mm (width)
	•	Door must open outwards (unless sliding) with a 900mm clear opening.
	•	Peninsular layout: Toilet pan 500mm from the side wall.
	•	Grab rails: Horizontal & vertical (contrast colour from wall)
	•	Clear transfer space on one side (minimum 1100mm)
	•	Washbasin reachable from WC (not in transfer space)
	•	Emergency alarm cord: red, reaches floor, reset button reachable from toilet.
	•	Mirror at appropriate height for seated & standing users. 
	•	Signage: Use accessible toilet symbol (and gender neutral if unisex)
Ambulant disabled cubicles (within standard toilets)
	•	Grab rails on both sides.
	•	Larger cubicle (minimum 800mm wide)
	•	Raised-height toilet seat.
	•	Outward-opening or sliding door preferable.
Extra things to consider
	•	Keep access routes clear. No bins or equipment blocking entry or transfer space.
	•	Maintain alarm cords. They should hang freely to the floor, not tied up!
	•	Ensure grab rails are secure, alarm systems functional, and lighting adequate.
	•	Use clear signage – visible, tactile (Braille or raised lettering), and with good contrast.

Parking and drop-off points
Provide accessible parking bays:
	•	Minimum bay width: 3.6m (2.4m + 1.2m side transfer zone).
	•	Located as close as possible to an accessible entrance.
	•	Provide a covered drop-off zone at main entrances where possible.
Meetings and events
	•	Choose venues that meet Part M accessibility standards.
	•	Ensure hearing loops are available and tested before events.
	•	Provide step-free access to all meeting rooms and breakout areas.
Quick accessibility checker 
	•	Step-free access to building and key areas. 
	•	Accessible toilets available and correctly fitted.
	•	Adequate space for wheelchair manoeuvring in all areas. 
	•	Clear, high-contrast signage with tactile/Braille options. 
	•	Lifts that meet Part M dimensions and controls guidance.
	•	Office furniture layout allows free movement.
	•	Lighting and acoustics are inclusive.
	•	Accessible parking provided.
