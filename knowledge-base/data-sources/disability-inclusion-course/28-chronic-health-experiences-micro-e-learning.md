### Video transcript: Long term health conditions

Narrator Meghan:

Did you know that an estimated 70 to 80% of disabled people don't have a visible impairment? This means you can't immediately tell what a person is experiencing or that they're disabled just by looking at them. This is true for many long-term health experiences, such as chronic pain or chronic fatigue.

Unfortunately, non-visible disabilities are often stigmatised in the workplace because there's often a question of needing to validate or prove your disability. Individuals with long-term health conditions and non-visible disability often struggle for many years to receive an appropriate medical diagnosis, and in the meantime are often not considered disabled enough to receive vital support in the workplace.

However, just because someone struggles to access a diagnosis if they choose to pursue that, doesn't mean that they aren't &nbsp;experiencing pain and therefore facing extra barriers to participating at work. In fact, Unison states 12 million sick days are lost every year in the UK to back pain alone, which is just one type of long-term condition.

This acts as a really important reminder of how absenteeism and high staff turnover can skyrocket when employees aren't given adequate support. When it comes to adjustments for long-term health, flexibility is key. In this section we'll explain how adaptable working environments, adaptable and flexible working hours, breaks, and clear manageable workloads can help create inclusive workspaces that support both wellbeing and performance.

Non-visible differences: common experiences
Chronic and long term health experiences often fall under the character of non-visible disabilities, and encompass a wide range of conditions.

Common experiences of chronic and long term health experiences include:
Pain
Persistent or fluctuating pain that can impact concentration, mobility, and daily tasks.

Dexterity
Spasms and other forms of pain can cause challenges with finger dexterity and fine motor controlled movements.

Burnout
A state of exhaustion caused by chronic stress, masking, or lack of support. Burnout can be long-lasting and often misunderstood.

Fatigue
Ongoing tiredness or exhaustion that rest doesn’t fully resolve, no matter how much sleep or rest you get.

Brain fog
Differences with memory, focus, or processing information quickly.

Fluctuations
Individuals will often experience good days and bad days as their condition or symptoms fluctuate.

## Image: text aside

Illustration of a woman jumping over a barrier

Common barriers

- Rigid working hours – strict start/finish times don’t account for fluctuating energy and pain levels.

- High cognitive demands – long meetings, or mentally intense tasks without breaks.

- Lack of ergonomic equipment - standard office equipment like fixed desks and poor seating may exacerbate pain levels.

- Lack of rest spaces – few or no quiet areas to recharge during the day.

- Stigma and misunderstanding – colleagues may misinterpret fatigue, brain fog, and withdrawal from social interaction&nbsp;as laziness or lack of motivation.

- Inflexible sick leave policies – not accounting for fluctuating conditions that may require occasional time off or reduced hours.

## Image: text aside

Illustration of a man with a blue bubble around him

Top Tips for supporting chronic health in the workplace

- Flexible scheduling – allow adjusted start/finish times, remote work, job shares, or part-time options when needed.

- Task prioritisation and pacing – help employees focus on high-value tasks, break work into smaller chunks, and avoid unnecessary multitasking.

- Rest and recovery spaces – provide quiet rooms or designated areas for short breaks to manage energy levels.

- Promoting the use of ergonomic equipment and assistive technology

- Awareness and training – educate teams about chronic health experiences&nbsp;to reduce stigma and foster empathy.

- Adaptive leave policies – ensure sick leave and absence frameworks recognise fluctuating conditions and allow time for recovery without penalty.
