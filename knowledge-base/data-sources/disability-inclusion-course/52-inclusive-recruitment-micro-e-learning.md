### Video transcript: adjustments within the recruitment process

Jane:

It's important to think about what adjustments a disabled candidate might need in the recruitment process. It will be different for everyone, but we need to look at each stage of the recruitment process to see what barriers might occur. So things like where you advertise, what you say in the ad advertisement, how you offer adjustments, how people are able to ask for adjustments, and they will be many and varied.

There are some things that we can anticipate. For example, a lot of disabled people and actually non-disabled people would prefer to have the questions in advance of an interview. Having five people fire random questions at you in an interview is hardly going to make you perform at your best. So one of the things that you could think about doing is offering the questions in advance to every candidate so that they don't need to actually make a request for those adjustments, but there will be other adjustments that individuals might need.

It might mean they need assistive technology, if they're going to be doing a particular assessment on, let's say a computer. It might mean they need a sign language interpreter if they're deaf. So the thing is to make sure that at every stage of the recruitment process, we offer candidates the opportunity to ask for any adjustments they might need for the next stage in the process, and then do what we can to make sure those adjustments are accommodated.

## Knowledgecheck: multiple choice

### You’re leading a hiring panel. A candidate asks if interview questions can be provided in advance so they have time to prepare.

C is correct because inclusive recruitment is about designing processes that reduce unnecessary barriers. Sharing questions with all candidates is fair, transparent, and gives everyone the best chance to show their skills.

- Answer option: A. Decline the request—everyone should experience the same conditions.

- Answer option: B. Provide the questions only to that candidate so their request is met.

- **Correct answer:** C. Share the questions with all candidates in advance, so everyone has adequate preparation time and nobody is disadvantaged.

## Knowledgecheck: multiple choice

### Which of the following best describes the goal of inclusive recruitment?

Correct answer! Inclusive recruitment is about proactively removing barriers and creating fair opportunities for all candidates to demonstrate their potential.

- Answer option: A. To make the recruitment process faster and more competitive.

- **Correct answer:** B. To identify and remove barriers at every stage so all candidates can show their true potential.

- Answer option: C. To focus only on attracting candidates with the most experience.

- Answer option: D. To increase the number of applications regardless of suitability.

## Knowledgecheck: multiple choice

### Which of the following is an example of an inclusive interview practice?

- **Correct answer:** A. Offering flexible interview formats and providing questions in advance.

- Answer option: B. Only conducting in-person interviews to gauge professionalism.

- Answer option: C. Keeping interview questions secret to test adaptability.

- Answer option: D. Using unstructured interviews to make candidates feel relaxed.

## Knowledgecheck: multiple choice

### Why should job descriptions focus on essential skills rather than long lists of requirements?

Correct answer! Simplifying job descriptions encourages more diverse applicants to apply, reducing unintentional barriers caused by overly detailed or exclusionary criteria.

- Answer option: A. Because it makes the role sound more impressive.

- Answer option: B. Because it ensures only qualified candidates apply.

- **Correct answer:** C. Because disabled and neurodivergent applicants are less likely to apply unless they meet 100% of the criteria.

- Answer option: D. Because fewer requirements make the job easier to advertise.
