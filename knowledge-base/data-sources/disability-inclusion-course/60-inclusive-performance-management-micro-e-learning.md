### Video transcript: Managing the performance of disabled employees

Jane:

I think a number of managers are quite nervous around managing the performance of disabled employees. We absolutely should manage the performance of disabled employees. It's not fair not to, because we all want development as well. However, we need to make sure that the performance that's being talked about isn't impacted by some kind of adjustment not being put in place.

So the first conversation with someone who may not be performing is, do you have everything you need in your, in your job? Is there something that I need to be providing that you need as as an adjustment or accommodation? Are you facing barriers which are preventing you from performing at the rate that you want to?

That's the first conversation to have. And it may be that the person isn't performing because they don't have the right technology, or they don't have the right instructions in the way that they would like it, or they're not given enough time to do the job that they're given to do, in which case, managing their performance is about providing those adjustments so that they can perform.

However, if we have provided absolutely every adjustment that we can and somebody still isn't performing, then we do go down the performance management route because nobody's exempt from that. And we just need to make sure that at every stage of the proceedings, that the adjustments that that person needs are in place.

If they are, then performance can be managed in the same way that it would with every other employee.

## Knowledgecheck: multiple choice

### What should a manager do first when they notice a potential performance issue with a disabled or neurodivergent employee?

- Answer option: A. Begin a formal performance process immediately.

- **Correct answer:** B. Check for barriers or missing workplace adjustments before taking formal steps.

- Answer option: C. Compare their performance directly to others in the team.

- Answer option: D. Assume the employee is not motivated.

## Knowledgecheck: multiple choice

### Which of the following best represents inclusive goal setting?

- Answer option: A. Focusing only on attendance and visibility in meetings.

- Answer option: B. Avoiding clear targets to reduce pressure.

- Answer option: C. Setting identical goals for all team members to ensure fairness.

- **Correct answer:** D. Creating flexible, measurable goals that are co-designed with the employee.

## Knowledgecheck: multiple choice

### What makes feedback truly inclusive and effective?

Correct answer! Inclusive feedback considers individual communication preferences and encourages dialogue, helping employees feel supported and involved in problem-solving.

- Answer option: A. Giving it in the same format to everyone for consistency.

- Answer option: B. Delivering it only when issues arise.

- **Correct answer:** C. Tailoring it to the person’s communication preferences and making it a two-way conversation.

- Answer option: D. Avoiding discussion of challenges to prevent discomfort.
