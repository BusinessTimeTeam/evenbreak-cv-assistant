# Accessible and inclusive recruitment practices

### Accessible job descriptions and applications

Write job adverts in plain, jargon-free language that focuses on essential skills rather than unnecessary requirements or past experience. Ensure application platforms are compatible with assistive technologies—for example, screen readers and dictation software—and offer alternative formats on request.

### Make adjustment intentions explicit

Clearly state in job adverts and communications that candidates can request adjustments at every stage of recruitment, including application, assessment and interview. Provide examples, such as offering a variety of times or locations or providing extra time for assessments.

### Inclusive interview and assessment practices

Provide interview questions in advance to all candidates to allow processing and preparation time. Use structured interviews with consistent questions to reduce bias. Offer flexible formats, such as remote interviews or written or practical alternatives, and avoid unnecessarily stressful or sensory-heavy environments.

### Train your hiring panel

Train recruiters and interviewers in unconscious bias, disability inclusion and neurodiversity awareness. Diverse panels help produce fairer decisions and reduce assumptions about what competence looks like.

### Supportive onboarding

Onboarding should include accessible induction materials, introductions to employee networks—for example, disability or neurodiversity employee resource groups—and proactive check-ins about adjustments. A buddy or mentor system can help new hires feel supported and integrated.
