# Performance management stage 2: constructive feedback

### Stage 2: giving feedback that builds confidence

Feedback is most effective when it is constructive, accessible and sensitive to the individual's needs and preferences.

**Consider communication preferences.** Some employees may prefer a conversation where they can ask questions and hear examples, while others may prefer written feedback so they do not have to process it on the spot in front of you.

**Balance strengths and areas for growth.** Acknowledge achievements and discuss areas for improvement sensitively, without judgemental language or accusations.

**Provide space for dialogue.** Feedback should be a two-way conversation where employees can explain how barriers may have affected their performance and suggest what could help.

**Important:** discuss challenges or concerns supportively. If you have noticed an employee missing deadlines, for example, you could say: “I've seen deadlines have been tricky lately. Are there adjustments we can put in place to make workloads more manageable?”

For more information about feedback in inclusive performance management, consult the performance management guide.
