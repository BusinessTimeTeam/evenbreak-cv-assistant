# Inclusive communication

Inclusive communication A best practice guide from Evenbreak
 
Introduction

This guide is for anyone creating or sharing information in a workplace setting, whether you're writing internal newsletters, posting on social media, building presentations, or having everyday conversations with colleagues. It offers straightforward actions to embed inclusive communication into your day-to-day work. 
Designed for everyday use, this guide will support you to:

	•	Communicate clearly, accessibly and inclusively with all colleagues.
	•	Make written and digital content more inclusive and WCAG-aligned.
	•	Create accessible emails, slide decks, newsletters, and internal documents.
	•	Share content on social media in ways that are inclusive and representative.
	•	Adapt communication styles to meet diverse preferences and access needs.  
Inclusive Written Content and Information Sharing Be clear
	•	Use plain language.
	•	Use short sentences and paragraphs.
	•	Stick to everyday words and avoid unnecessary jargon or acronyms (if you must use them, spell them out the first time).

Make content easy to scan
	•	Use descriptive subheadings. 
	•	Break up text with bullet points and numbered lists.
	•	Bold key information, but avoid using colour alone to show importance.

Make key info easy to find
	•	Put the most important message first.
	•	Include summaries or TL;DR (Too Long; Didn’t Read) sections at the top.
	•	Use consistent formatting (e.g., headers, font size) across documents.

Design for everyone
	•	Use high-contrast colours (e.g. black on pale grey, not pale grey on white.
	•	Minimum font size: 12pt, and 18pt presentations.
	•	Use a sans-serif font like Arial, Calibri, or Verdana.  
WCAG-aligned accessible digital content
 The WebContentAccessibilityGuidelines (WCAG2.1) are the gold standard for ensuring digital content is accessible to everyone including people who are blind or visually impaired, neurodivergent, or have mobility or cognitive differences. 
These guidelines apply across all workplace content. That includes:
	•	Slide decks 
	•	Intranet content 
	•	PDFs and documents 
	•	Newsletters
	•	Social media posts
	•	Emails

Below are the key WCAG principles, with specific requirements and practical actions to embed them in everyday communication. 
Headings and structure
	•	Use Heading 1 (H1) for your page title, then Heading 2 (H2) for main sections, Heading 3 (H3) for subsections, etc.
	•	Use built-in heading styles in Word, PowerPoint, SharePoint, or HTML. 
Alt-text
	•	Add concise alt text (up to 125 characters) for images that convey information (e.g."Bar chart showing employee satisfaction scores across 4 quarters").
	•	Decorative images should be marked as decorative (in Word, PowerPoint, or HTML).
 
Colour contrast Use contrast ratios that meet WCAG Level AA, which are:
	•	4.5:1 for normal text (under 18pt or 14pt bold)
	•	3:1 for large text (18pt+ or 14pt+ bold)
	•	Use free tools like WebAIM Contrast Checker to test combinations.
Fonts
	•	Use sans-serif fonts like Arial, Calibri, Tahoma, or Verdana.
	•	Avoid italics, underlines, or all caps for long text.
	•	Set body text to at least 12pt (preferably 14pt or higher).
	•	Use 1.5 line spacing and generous spacing between paragraphs. 
Descriptive links
	•	Use clear, meaningful link text, for example:
	•	Good: 'Download the 2025 Accessibility Report'
	•	Bad: 'Click here' or 'More info' 
Captions and transcriptions
	•	Add accurate captions to all video content (YouTube, Teams recordings, etc.).
	•	Always review and correct auto-captions.
	•	Provide transcripts for audio content (like podcasts or webinars).
	•	Accessible file formats
	•	Use Word or tagged PDFs, never share scanned PDFs (they're just images and not screen-readable).
	•	Use accessibility checkers before sharing (available in Microsoft Word, PowerPoint, and Adobe Acrobat).
	•	Avoid embedding text inside images as it cannot be read by screen readers.

Keyboard accessibility
	•	All interactive elements (buttons, forms, dropdowns) must be operable by keyboard alone.
	•	Test by navigating with Tab / Shift+Tab / Enter. If you can't access something this way, it's not accessible.
	•	Test and review your content.
	•	Use built-in accessibility checkers in Microsoft 365 and Adobe.
	•	Use free tools like: WAVE WebAIM Colour Contrast Checker.
	•	Ask disabled colleagues or employee networks for feedback.  
Quick checklist to embed WCAG principles in your team's workflow:
	•	All content has clearly labelled headings.
	•	Images have alt text or are marked decorative.
	•	Text meets minimum colour contrast.
	•	Fonts are sans-serif and minimum 12pt.
	•	All links are descriptive.
	•	Videos have captions / transcripts.
	•	Files are tested for screen reader accessibility.
	•	Interactive content works without a mouse.
	•	Feedback from disabled users informs content design. 
Inclusive Internal Comms (Emails, Slides, Documents, Newsletters)
Emails 
	•	Use a clear subject line that captures the purpose.
	•	Keep emails short and use bullet points or spacing.
	•	Highlight actions and deadlines.
	•	Offer an alternative format if needed (e.g. voice note, follow-up call).

Slides and presentations
	•	Use high contrast and readable fonts.
	•	Don't overload slides, aim for one three short bullet points per slide.
	•	Verbally describe any visual content during live presentations.
	•	Share slides in advance. 
Newsletters or bulletins
	•	Provide plain-text versions.
	•	Structure content with headings, summaries and visual breaks. 
	•	Keep layouts simple, with good colour contrast and accessible formats (e.g. avoid PDFs unless tagged for accessibility).

Inclusive Social Media
Visual content
	•	Always use alt-text for images.
	•	Don't put text inside images without repeating it in the caption or post.
	•	Avoid flashing content and use animation carefully. 
Hashtags and emojis
	•	Capitalise hashtags using CamelCase (e.g. #DisabilityInclusion, not #disabilityinclusion).
	•	Limit emoji use, and place them at the end of posts, not mid -sentence. 
Videos and audio
	•	Add open or closed captions.
	•	Use auto-caption tools carefully and edit them for accuracy.
	•	Offer a transcript or short written summary for key messages.

Tone and representation
	•	Avoid charity model and deficit-based language (e.g. "suffering from…").
	•	Amplify voices from underrepresented group, tag creators and give credit. Inclusive Interactions

Respect different communication preferences
	•	Ask: "How do you prefer to communicate?".
	•	Some may prefer written messages, some verbal, some visual tools.
	•	Don't assume someone's comfort with video calls, phone calls, or fast- paced discussions.

Active listening and clarity
	•	Give people time to think and respond. Don't interrupt or rush.
	•	Repeat key points or summarise to check understanding.
	•	Avoid idioms and metaphors that may be unclear (e.g."hit the ground running"). 
Meetings
	•	Share agendas in advance.
	•	Build in quiet thinking time before asking for responses.
	•	Offer alternative ways to contribute (e.g. follow-up via chat or email). 
Identity and access needs
	•	Use people's names and pronouns correctly.
	•	Don't pressure people to speak or participate beyond their comfort level.
	•	Ensure assistive technology works in the environment.

Building a Culture of Inclusive Communication  Normalise asking what works
	•	"What's the best way for me to share information with you?"
	•	"Do you prefer written follow-ups or conversations?"
	•	"Would a visual, audio, or written version be helpful?"

Make inclusive comms a team norm
	•	Add a team-wide "communication preferences" document.
	•	Share inclusive comms guidance in onboarding packs.
	•	Encourage peer feedback on how communications land.

Be open to feedback and improvement
If someone tells you your communication wasn't accessible, thank them. Fix it and make a note to change future practice. Inclusion is a learning process, progress is better than perfection!
 Closing Thoughts Communication is Culture 
 How we communicate shapes how people feel at work. Inclusive communication isn't about ticking boxes, it's about building trust, connection, and equity. 
When we communicate accessibly and inclusively, we're enabling everyone to thrive.
