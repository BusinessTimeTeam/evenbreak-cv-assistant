### Video transcript: Rele's experience

Rele:

Hi, my name is Rele and I'm an account manager at Evenbreak, and I have a spinal injury which was caused by a car accident, level C5 incomplete. So that means that I have partial paralysis in all four limbs and I have some, I have full sensation from head to toe. So being spinely injured means that I use a manual wheelchair to move around for mobility.

And barriers I've faced when applying for work or the workplace environment are, I need a workplace that has wheelchair accessibility, you know, that I can fit my manual wheelchair through the door frame. It needs to have parking. That's something to take into consideration and most importantly has to have a wheelchair accessible toilet so that I can use it during the day.

Barriers that I've faced when applying are, not knowing whether the interview, where I can go for an interview will be accessible. I always have to notify the organisation I'm applying to, whether it'll be accessible, a toilet, an accessible toilet, a parking space, no steps access or a ramp, that kind of stuff.

Adjustments that's worked well for me in my previous occupations, in my previous jobs I've had, some doors in the hallway were made accessible for me by having a push button where the doors, you push the button and the doors open on their own. 'Cause I'm not able to open a door manually by myself in the wheelchair.

And that also means I don't need to wait for someone to open the door on my behalf. Another adjustment that's worked well for me would be a desk that's got a button that can move up and down. So I'm able to see my computer screen and I can adjust the height of the table so that my knees can fit under the table because a lot of tables I've found my knees don't fit underneath in the wheelchair.

So that's another thing that's worked for me, an adjustment. Also, another adjustment I've had is when I'm working, let's say we're doing an assessment, for example, because I haven't got much dexterity in my hands my writing is quite slow. So I need more time to complete a test, for example.

So that's an adjustment that's been made for me before. Small things that make a big difference for me in terms of colleagues or managers support is, if we are going to a team event or any team meetings, just to make sure the room is accessible, wheelchair accessible. Or the venue that we were going to, if we're going on a team away day, has wheelchair accessible toilets.

That's always important. And also if there is any accessible parking, disabled parking, that would be helpful as well. And colleagues just generally knowing that, for example, in the office space, not to put anything in my way or obstacles I'm not able to get past in my wheelchair. Try not to use the disabled toilet if possible, because I might need to use it at a certain time.

And yeah, just to be open-minded on different ways of doing things and being patient, because I do things a bit slower than other people might do. Just things like that. Okay. Thank you.
